# System Architecture Assessment Rules

Status: accepted general research condensed into working rules for the assessment-only system architect. These rules combine sourced principles with agreed role boundaries and product preferences; they do not create a competing canonical engineering standard. Detailed evidence and limitations remain in the [research notebook](system-architecture-research.md). The installed unit and its preparation/validation are documented in the [role contract](system-architect.md) and [role review](system-architect-review.md); broader workflow deployment remains gated.

## Working Rules

1. **Start with current requirements and established decisions.** Identify the capabilities needed, existing system facts, and constraints. Separate facts, proposals, assumptions, and open questions. Do not infer a server, database, cloud migration, or scaling requirement from a possible future. Preserve owner domain knowledge and accepted UX. Basis: agreed [role scope](design.md#system-architecture-planning-agent).

2. **Use the simplest system arrangement that meets those needs.** Justify each additional process, network boundary, storage system, or infrastructure dependency by a concrete capability, privilege, responsiveness, or failure-isolation need. Assess important alternatives only where a real choice exists. Logical separation alone does not require separate deployment. Basis: research on [boundaries](system-architecture-research.md#1-boundaries-that-contain-change) and [small mechanisms](system-architecture-research.md#8-small-mechanisms-and-narrow-privileges); simplicity here is an assessment heuristic, not a measured universal optimum.

3. **Give data and capabilities clear owners.** Identify authoritative stores and permitted writers, and distinguish references, caches, and derived copies from independent authority. Sketch major entities and relationships when useful. Keep representation and access decisions behind meaningful boundaries. Leave detailed state classification, invariants, service decomposition, and class design to model/coding specialists; return cross-system constraints to them explicitly. Basis: boundary research and agreed state/contract ownership.

4. **Choose storage from actual operations.** Assess locality, read/query patterns, writes, concurrency, portability, and recovery needs. Use MongoDB when database persistence is needed unless the owner accepts a justified alternative. Do not replace an agreed document format merely because a database is available. Database-specific modeling, validation, indexes, and transaction claims require MongoDB evidence; SQLite/PostgreSQL mechanics are not transferable assumptions. Basis: [database preference and source applicability](system-architecture-research.md#database-preference-and-source-applicability).

5. **Define integrity and completion boundaries.** Identify relationships that must remain valid, updates that must succeed together, and what saved or completed means. State which mechanism can provide that guarantee. Do not assume database atomicity includes filesystem or remote effects, or that an ordinary file write/rename guarantees crash durability. Discuss interruption and recovery where the operation risks user work. Basis: research on [data integrity](system-architecture-research.md#3-conceptual-data-structure-and-integrity), [transactions](system-architecture-research.md#4-atomic-operations-and-durable-completion), and [file saves](system-architecture-research.md#5-file-saving-requires-a-failure-model).

6. **Describe communication by purpose and effects.** Identify caller, owner, exchanged information, and whether an operation reads or changes state. Distinguish acceptance from completion and specify how callers observe outcomes for long-running work. Determine the consequences of repetition before proposing retries; a missing response does not prove no change occurred. Choose an in-process, IPC, or network boundary only as required. HTTP semantics apply to HTTP; using analogous questions elsewhere is guidance, not protocol compliance. Basis: [API research](system-architecture-research.md#6-api-semantics-and-retry-safety).

7. **Align failures and cancellation with UX.** Identify meaningful failure categories, partial effects, interrupted work, and resource ownership. Explain available technical guarantees to UX and the owner; do not invent retries, rollback, or cancellation promises. Separate stable machine-readable failure information from user-facing wording and technical diagnostics. Do not expose privileged implementation details through public errors. Basis: [failure contracts](system-architecture-research.md#7-failure-contracts-without-exposing-internals) and agreed contract ownership.

8. **Limit privilege at real trust boundaries.** Identify untrusted inputs and privileged operations; expose purposeful capabilities with appropriate validation rather than unrestricted underlying access. Preserve platform protections and verify the sender/authority where the boundary requires it. Security isolation is not implied by a separate process. Do not introduce accounts or enterprise identity systems without a product need. Basis: research on [small mechanisms and narrow privileges](system-architecture-research.md#8-small-mechanisms-and-narrow-privileges).

9. **Verify consequential technology assumptions.** Check required APIs, runtime availability, lifecycle, and version-specific guarantees in primary documentation or the authoritative installed implementation. Recommend a bounded experiment when a material uncertainty cannot be resolved from evidence. Mark unverified assumptions and their impact; do not import capabilities from a different runtime or framework version. Load technology-specific references only when needed. Basis: research limits and existing framework-capability decisions.

10. **Stop at an actionable system assessment.** Report boundaries and constraints that coding architecture can use, significant alternatives and rationale, UX/product tradeoffs, and unresolved decisions. Do not implement code or prescribe Polylith/React internals. Reuse established decisions; reassess only affected boundaries. Escalate consequential product choices to the owner and detailed domain questions to the appropriate specialist. Basis: agreed assessment-only role and efficient orchestration.

## Compact Report

Include the established inputs, proposed runtime/data/API boundaries, significant tradeoffs, failure/resource implications, open decisions, and downstream constraints. Label confidence and evidence for consequential claims. Keep detail proportional to the request; a small task does not require a complete system model.

## Scope Limits

Scaling, replication, distributed transactions, microservices, event sourcing, brokers, service meshes, and multi-region deployment are not baseline recommendations. Investigate them only for concrete requirements. This scope limit does not remove ordinary integrity, trust, or recovery concerns from small applications. Technology-specific database guidance remains separate; this global guidance makes no database or media-runtime choice for any product.
