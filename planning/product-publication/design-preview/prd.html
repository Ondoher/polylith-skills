Fieldbook / Product definition

Product requirements

A dependable record of each harvest, ready to revisit throughout the growing season.

Illustrative draftDocument revision 2Format proposal 02

1 Product overview

Gardeners need to record what they harvested and when, without turning a brief note into a bookkeeping task. Fieldbook gives each harvest a dated entry and preserves that entry for later reference.

The first release centers on recording and revisiting harvests. Its value depends on a simple promise: a note shown as saved remains available when the gardener returns.

The product promise

Capture a useful observation, know whether it was saved, and find it again later.

2 Users, goals, and success

The primary user is a home gardener recording a harvest during or soon after working outdoors. The product supports a short recording task and a later review of those observations.

User goal Expected outcome
Record today's harvest A dated, non-empty entry appears in the harvest history after saving succeeds.
Return to an earlier observation Previously saved entries are available after reopening the application.

No adoption or time-to-complete target is specified in this illustrative brief. Those measures remain an explicit open decision.

3 Scope and boundaries

3.1 In scope

  • Record a dated harvest note.
  • Review saved harvest entries.
  • Reject empty submissions and recover from unsuccessful saves.

3.2 Outside this example's scope

  • Sharing or collaborative editing.
  • Harvest forecasts and recommendations.
  • Automatic weather or sensor data.

The application is described for one gardener. Account, device-sync, and collaboration behavior are not implied by this example.

4 Product concepts

Harvest entry
A dated observation recorded by the gardener.
Draft
The current entry before a save has succeeded.
Harvest history
The collection of entries that have been successfully saved.

5 Functional requirements

5.1 Recording and returning to harvests

Recording must preserve the distinction between an entry being edited and one that is safely saved. The following requirements govern that boundary.

REQ-01

5.1.1 Record a dated harvest

Fieldbook must let the gardener save a harvest entry containing a date and a non-empty note.

Acceptance criteria
  1. Before saving, the gardener can review the date and note and return to edit without losing either value. Review alone creates no saved entry.
  2. Saving a valid reviewed entry adds it to the harvest history with the reviewed date and note.
  3. The interface reports success only after the save succeeds.

Interaction: UC-01 · Record a harvest (§1.2.1.1)

Source and decision record

REQ-02

5.1.2 Prevent empty entries

Fieldbook must reject a save when the harvest note is empty or contains only whitespace.

Acceptance criteria
  1. An empty submission creates no saved entry.
  2. The gardener can correct the note and try again without re-entering the date.

Interaction: UC-01, branch 2a · Empty note

REQ-03

5.1.3 Preserve saved entries

Fieldbook must retain successfully saved harvest entries after the application is closed and reopened.

Acceptance criteria
  1. Reopening the application shows the saved date and note without alteration.

Related flow: Returning to harvest history (§1.2.1.1.3)

6 Quality and constraints

REQ-04 · Reliability

6.1 Keep the draft when saving fails

If a save fails, Fieldbook must retain the entered date and note, disclose the failure, and let the gardener retry. The failed attempt must not be presented as a saved entry.

Acceptance criterion

The draft remains available after a failed save, and retrying can complete that same entry.

Interaction: UC-01, branch 5a · Save failure

Accessibility targets, performance thresholds, and supported platforms are not specified in the sample. They must be resolved in product sources before being stated as accepted requirements.

7 Acceptance and dependencies

The core scenario is complete when a gardener enters a harvest, reviews and corrects its details, saves it, sees confirmation, closes the application, and returns to the same saved entry. This connects the recording requirement to the persistence requirement.

Acceptance also exercises empty-note correction and a failed save followed by a successful retry. Detailed interface behavior is described in Record a harvest (§1.2.1.1). No external service dependency is specified in this sample.
